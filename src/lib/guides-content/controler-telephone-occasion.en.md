# How to inspect a used phone before buying

The used phone market is full of opportunities — and traps. A phone that looks intact can hide a worn battery or a software lock that makes it unusable. This checklist covers everything to verify before paying.

## 1. Check the IMEI number

Ask the seller for the IMEI number before meeting (it displays by dialing `*#06#`). It is the device's unique fingerprint.

Make sure the number shown on screen matches the one printed on the box if there is one, and the one in settings. Any difference means changed parts or a dubious origin — walk away immediately.

You can also check the device status on free IMEI check websites to make sure it has not been reported stolen to manufacturers.

## 2. Battery health

The battery is the component that degrades most over time, and its replacement is expensive on some models.

- **iPhone**: Settings → Battery → Battery Health. Above 85% is good; below 80%, a replacement will soon be needed.
- **Android**: some devices show battery health in settings; otherwise, watch the discharge speed during inspection — a phone that loses 10% in a few minutes usually has a worn battery.

Ask the seller the phone's age and charging habits — permanent overnight charging accelerates degradation.

## 3. Screen and touch

Inspect the screen on a white then black background: look for yellow spots, dead pixel lines and burn-in traces (common on OLED screens where icon shadows remain).

Test touch across the entire surface — drag an icon to every corner and edge. Any area that does not respond signals a faulty digitizer, an expensive repair.

Check the fingerprint sensor or face recognition, as well as auto-brightness (cover the top sensor and watch it dim).

## 4. Buttons, cameras and sound

Try each physical button: power, volume (up and down), silent mode. Sunken buttons or ones requiring hard pressure betray wear.

Open the front and rear cameras, take a photo and a video: note sharpness, autofocus, absence of dark spots on the lens (internal dust or past moisture). Test the flash.

Make a test call: check the earpiece, speaker and microphone — speak and ask your caller if your voice is clear.

## 5. iCloud lock and Google account — the most critical point

This is the most important point on the entire list. A phone tied to the previous owner's account can become unusable.

- **iPhone**: make sure "Find My iPhone" is disabled and the iCloud sign-out is done **in front of you** before payment. Ask the seller to enter their passcode to complete the sign-out — if they refuse or claim to have forgotten, do not buy.
- **Android**: verify the Google account removal (Settings → Accounts). The FRP lock will prevent you from using the phone after a reset if the seller's account remains registered.

Perform a factory reset in front of the seller and verify the phone boots cleanly without asking for the previous owner's credentials. This single step protects you from most used-phone scams.

## In summary

Do not rush. Spend 15 minutes on the full inspection and pay no deposit before examining. An honest seller welcomes a thorough inspection — a reluctant seller is usually hiding something.
