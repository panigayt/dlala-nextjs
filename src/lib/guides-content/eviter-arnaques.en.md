# How to avoid scams in classified ads

Classified ads are an excellent way to buy and sell, but they also attract scammers. The good news: most scams follow repetitive patterns, easy to detect once you know them. This guide gives you the warning signs and essential safety rules.

## Warning signs: when to walk away immediately

- **An illogical price**: a car or phone far below market price is usually not a "good deal" — it is bait. The scammer counts on your greed to lower your guard.
- **Refusal to meet**: a seller who refuses in-person inspection and only offers shipping, or who delays showing the item — a clear warning sign.
- **Advance payment requests**: any request for a transfer to "confirm your seriousness" or "reserve the item" before inspection is an almost certain scam.
- **Missing documents**: a car seller who dodges the registration card in their name, or a phone seller who refuses to sign out of their account in front of you.

## The safe meeting rule

Always meet in a **public, busy place**: a well-known café, a public square, or in front of a police station. Avoid homes and isolated places, especially for large sums.

Tell a trusted person the meeting place and your expected return time. For expensive purchases (cars), come accompanied — a witness's presence discourages most scammers.

Prefer daytime meetings. If the other party insists on nighttime or a dubious place, cancel without hesitation.

## Never pay in advance

This rule has no exceptions: **transfer no money before inspecting the item and verifying the documents**. No deposit, no "reservation fees", no "shipping fees".

Scammers excel at pretexts: "I am in a distant wilaya", "I will send you the item after the transfer". These are documented methods to steal your money with no recourse.

Pay only on receipt after full inspection — and preferably by a traceable method (bank transfer) for high amounts.

## Verify documents and identity

- **Cars**: registration card in the seller's name, quitus, chassis number match. See our complete used-car buying guide.
- **Phones**: iCloud/Google sign-out in front of you and factory reset. See our used-phone inspection guide.
- **Real estate**: land registry or title deed, no disputes — consult a notary for large amounts.

Ask to see the seller's ID and compare the name with the documents. A serious seller will not object; the scammer will invent excuses.

## What to do if you are a scam victim?

- Keep all evidence: conversation screenshots, phone numbers, transfer receipts.
- File a complaint with the security services with all the evidence.
- Report the fraudulent account to the platform's administration to protect others.
- Alert local groups if the scammer is active there — sticking to facts, without unfounded defamation.

## In summary

Trust is built through verification, not words. A genuine seller understands your caution and welcomes inspection and documents — and faced with someone who rushes you or dodges verification, walking away is a gain, not a loss.
